California-Specific Considerations When Selling As-Is

Navigating California Tenant Laws

If your Costa Mesa property has tenants, California's renter-friendly laws can complicate traditional sales. The state's strict tenant protections, including rent control provisions in some areas and just-cause eviction requirements, often make properties less attractive to conventional buyers.

Cash buyers experienced in California real estate understand these complexities and can purchase properties with tenants in place, managing the situation according to state and local regulations.

Understanding California's Disclosure Requirements

California requires extensive property disclosures, including the Transfer Disclosure Statement (TDS) and Natural Hazard Disclosure (NHD). While you're still required to disclose known issues when selling as-is, cash buyers are typically more accepting of properties with disclosed problems that might scare away traditional buyers.

Property Tax Implications

Under California's Proposition 13, your property taxes are based on your purchase price with limited annual increases. When you sell, the new owner's property taxes reset to current market value. While this doesn't directly affect your sale proceeds, understanding this helps you appreciate why some buyers hesitate—but cash buyers factor this into their offers and move forward anyway.

The California Escrow Process

California uses an escrow system for real estate transactions, where a neutral third party holds funds and documents until all conditions are met. Cash sales still go through escrow, but without loan contingencies, the process moves much faster—typically 7-14 days versus 30-60 days for financed purchases.
