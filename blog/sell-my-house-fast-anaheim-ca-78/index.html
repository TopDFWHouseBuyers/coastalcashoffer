California Tenant Laws Make Traditional Sales Complicated

California has some of the strongest tenant protections in the country. Under the Tenant Protection Act of 2019 (AB 1482), you must provide 'just cause' for eviction in many cases, and tenants have significant rights during a property sale. If you have tenants in place, you'll need to provide proper notice for showings, which can take months and create conflict.

Many traditional buyers won't purchase occupied rental properties, especially if tenants aren't cooperative. This dramatically limits your buyer pool and can cause deals to fall through at the last minute.

California-Specific Considerations When Selling Your Rental

Selling rental property in California involves unique factors that don't apply in other states. Understanding these helps explain why cash sales are particularly attractive for California landlords.

Proposition 19 and Property Tax Implications

California's property tax structure under Proposition 13 means your rental property may have a significantly lower tax basis than current market value. While this benefited you as an owner, it also means potential buyers will face much higher property taxes, which can affect your ability to sell at top dollar through traditional channels.

Lengthy Escrow Periods

Traditional California real estate transactions typically take 30-45 days minimum, often longer with financing contingencies, inspections, and appraisals. Cash sales through Golden Coast Cash Offer can close in as little as 7-14 days, getting you out from under the property much faster.

Disclosure Requirements

California requires extensive disclosures when selling property, and rental properties come with additional concerns about tenant histories, code violations, and property conditions. Cash buyers are experienced investors who understand these issues and won't nickel-and-dime you over every disclosure item.
