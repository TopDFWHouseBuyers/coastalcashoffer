California Probate Process for Selling a House Guide 2026

Selling a house through probate in California can feel overwhelming during an already difficult time. Whether you've inherited a property in Orange County, Los Angeles, or anywhere in Southern California, understanding the specific probate requirements will help you navigate this process with confidence and avoid costly mistakes.

California's probate laws are unique, and selling inherited real estate here involves specific court procedures, timelines, and costs that differ from other states. This guide walks you through everything you need to know about the California probate selling house process in 2026.

What Is Probate and When Is It Required in California?

Probate is the legal process of transferring property ownership after someone passes away. In California, probate is typically required when the deceased owned real estate solely in their name without beneficiary designations, transfer-on-death deeds, or trust arrangements.

California law sets a probate threshold at $184,500 for 2026 (adjusted periodically for inflation). If the estate's total value exceeds this amount, which most Southern California properties do given our high real estate values, full probate proceedings are necessary.

Properties That Must Go Through Probate

  • Single-family homes owned individually without a living trust
  • Investment properties or rental homes in the deceased's name
  • Properties owned as tenants-in-common (not joint tenancy)
  • Real estate without proper estate planning documents

Given that the median home price in Orange County exceeds $1 million and even modest properties in areas like Santa Ana or Garden Grove typically surpass $700,000, almost all inherited homes in Southern California require probate proceedings.

The California Probate Selling House Process: Step-by-Step

Step 1: Petition the Court and Get Appointed

The process begins by filing a petition with the Superior Court in the county where the deceased lived. In Orange County, this would be the Orange County Superior Court. The executor or administrator must be officially appointed by the court, which typically takes 30-60 days after filing.

Step 2: Notify Creditors and Heirs

California law requires a formal notification period where creditors have 120 days to file claims against the estate. Heirs and beneficiaries must also receive proper notice according to California Probate Code requirements.

Step 3: Obtain Court Permission to Sell

Unlike some states, California requires court confirmation for probate property sales unless the will specifically granted the executor Independent Administration of Estates Act (IAEA) authority. Most estate sales require filing a petition for court confirmation, which adds time and complexity to the selling process.

Step 4: List and Market the Property

Once authorized, you can list the property for sale. The executor must obtain a probate referee's appraisal to establish the property's value. In California, you typically must sell for at least 90% of the appraised value to avoid additional court hearings.

Step 5: Accept an Offer and Court Confirmation

After accepting an offer, you'll return to court for confirmation. Here's where California probate gets interesting: at the confirmation hearing, the court allows overbidding. Other buyers can outbid the original offer by at least 5% of the first $10,000 and smaller percentages thereafter. This process, while potentially increasing the sale price, creates uncertainty for buyers and can extend timelines.

Step 6: Close Escrow

Once the court confirms the sale, you'll proceed to close escrow, typically within 30-45 days. Southern California escrow companies experienced with probate sales will handle the specific requirements and documentation.

California Probate Costs: What to Expect

California has statutory fees for probate, meaning costs are set by law based on the estate's value. These fees can be substantial given Southern California's high property values.

Statutory Executor and Attorney Fees

Both the executor and attorney are entitled to fees calculated as:

  • 4% of the first $100,000
  • 3% of the next $100,000
  • 2% of the next $800,000
  • 1% of the next $9 million
  • 0.5% of the next $15 million

For a $1 million Orange County home, statutory fees would be $23,000 each for the executor and attorney—$46,000 total before considering additional costs.

Additional Probate Expenses

  • Court filing fees: $435-$500
  • Probate referee fees: 0.1% of appraised value
  • Publication costs: $200-$600 for required legal notices
  • Property maintenance, insurance, and taxes during probate
  • Real estate commissions (if using traditional listing)

Total probate costs often range from 5-10% of the estate's value in California, significantly higher than many other states.

Timeline for California Probate Property Sales

California probate is notoriously lengthy. Even straightforward cases typically take 9-18 months from start to finish. Complex estates, disputes among heirs, or complications with creditor claims can extend this to 2+ years.

Breaking down the timeline:

  • Court appointment of executor: 30-60 days
  • Creditor claim period: 4 months minimum
  • Property preparation and marketing: 1-3 months
  • Escrow and court confirmation: 45-90 days
  • Additional court hearings if needed: Variable

During this time, you're responsible for maintaining the property, paying property taxes (which in California can be reassessed upon transfer under Proposition 19 changes), insurance, utilities, and possibly dealing with existing tenants protected under California's strict tenant laws.

Special Considerations for Southern California Probate Sales

High Property Values and Tax Implications

Southern California's elevated real estate prices mean probate estates regularly exceed $1-2 million in value. Beneficiaries receive a stepped-up cost basis to fair market value at the date of death, which can minimize capital gains taxes. However, Proposition 19 (effective 2021) eliminated the parent-child property tax reassessment exclusion unless the heir uses it as their primary residence, creating additional considerations.

Inherited Properties with Tenants

If you've inherited a rental property in Irvine, Huntington Beach, or other Southern California cities with rent control or just cause eviction ordinances, you face additional complexities. California's tenant protection laws limit your ability to remove tenants, even as the new owner. Properties with tenant complications often benefit from selling to cash buyers experienced with occupied properties.

Property Condition and Market Timing

Many inherited homes in older neighborhoods of Anaheim, Santa Ana, Fullerton, or Costa Mesa haven't been updated in decades. The costs to repair, renovate, and bring properties to market condition can be substantial. During the lengthy probate process, deferred maintenance continues while you're paying carrying costs.

Alternatives and Shortcuts to Full Probate

Affidavit for Small Estates

If the entire estate (not just real property) is worth less than $184,500, California allows a simplified small estate affidavit process that avoids court proceedings entirely. This rarely applies to Southern California real estate given current values.

Spousal Property Petition

Surviving spouses can use a simplified Spousal Property Petition process rather than full probate administration, significantly reducing time and costs.

Independent Administration Authority

If granted IAEA powers, executors can sell property without court confirmation, eliminating the overbidding process and reducing timelines by 2-3 months. This requires specific language in the will or agreement from all beneficiaries.

Should You Sell the Probate Property As-Is?

Many executors face the dilemma of whether to invest in repairs and updates before selling. Consider these factors:

  • Carrying costs: Every month you hold the property costs money in taxes, insurance, utilities, and maintenance
  • Out-of-pocket expenses: Repairs require upfront cash from the estate or executor personally
  • Market timing: Southern California's real estate market fluctuates; delays risk value changes
  • Executor liability: Making wrong renovation decisions can expose you to beneficiary complaints

Selling as-is to a cash buyer eliminates these concerns entirely. You avoid renovation decisions, reduce carrying costs, and settle the estate faster.
