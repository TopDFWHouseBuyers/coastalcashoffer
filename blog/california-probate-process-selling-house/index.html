Understanding California Probate: What Makes It Different

California's probate process has unique requirements that set it apart from other states. When you inherit a house in Orange County, Los Angeles, or San Diego, you'll typically need court approval before selling unless the estate qualifies for special exceptions.

The California Probate Code governs the entire process, and understanding these specific laws can save you months of delays and thousands of dollars in unnecessary costs.

Does Every Inherited House Go Through Probate?

Not necessarily. Your inherited property may avoid probate if:

  • The property was held in a living trust
  • It was owned as joint tenants with right of survivorship
  • The estate value falls under $184,500 (California's small estate threshold)
  • The property was designated with a transfer-on-death deed

However, most single-family homes in Orange County, where median values exceed $1 million, will require formal probate proceedings.

The California Probate Timeline for Selling a House

One of the most frustrating aspects of probate in California is the timeline. While a normal home sale in Orange County might close in 30 days, probate sales typically take much longer.

Typical Timeline Breakdown

Here's what to expect when selling a house through California probate:

  • Month 1-2: Filing the petition with the Superior Court in your county (Orange County Superior Court handles local cases)
  • Month 2-4: Court hearing to appoint the executor or administrator
  • Month 4-6: Notifying creditors and beneficiaries (California requires a 4-month creditor claim period)
  • Month 6-8: Obtaining court authority to sell the property
  • Month 8-10: Marketing and accepting offers
  • Month 10-12: Court confirmation hearing and closing

In total, expect 9-12 months for a standard probate sale in California, though some cases extend even longer.

California Probate Court Approval Requirements

Unlike a regular sale, selling a house through California probate requires specific court procedures. Understanding these requirements helps you avoid costly mistakes.

Independent Administration vs. Full Authority

California offers two main types of probate administration:

Independent Administration of Estates Act (IAEA): This gives the executor more authority to sell real estate with minimal court supervision. You'll still need to follow notice requirements, but you can close the sale without a court confirmation hearing. This is the faster option.

Full Authority: Requires a court confirmation hearing where the judge must approve the sale. The court will also entertain overbids, meaning someone can outbid your buyer at the hearing by at least 5% of the first $10,000 and 10% of the amount above that.

Most attorneys recommend pursuing IAEA authority when possible to streamline the process.

California-Specific Costs of Selling Through Probate

Probate sales in California come with unique costs that eat into your inheritance. Understanding these expenses helps you make informed decisions.

Statutory Fees for Executors and Attorneys

California Probate Code Section 10810 sets specific compensation rates:

  • 4% of the first $100,000
  • 3% of the next $100,000
  • 2% of the next $800,000
  • 1% of the next $9 million
  • 0.5% of the next $15 million

For a typical Orange County home worth $1 million, this means $23,000 in executor fees and another $23,000 in attorney fees—$46,000 total before any other costs.

Additional Probate Expenses

Beyond statutory fees, expect to pay for:

  • Court filing fees ($435-$600)
  • Publication costs for creditor notices ($200-$500)
  • Property appraisal ($500-$800)
  • Bond premiums if required ($500-$2,000 annually)
  • Property maintenance, insurance, and taxes during probate
  • Standard selling costs (title, escrow, transfer taxes)

Orange County transfer taxes vary by city but typically range from $0.55 to $1.10 per $1,000 of sale price.

Preparing the Inherited Property for Sale

While navigating probate, you must also maintain and prepare the property. Southern California's competitive market means presentation matters, but you're in a unique position.

Property Condition Challenges

Many inherited homes in Orange County haven't been updated in decades. Your loved one may have lived there for 40+ years, benefiting from Proposition 13 tax protections, but leaving you with original 1970s finishes.

You face a dilemma: invest money in repairs and updates, or sell as-is? Consider that:

  • Renovations cost more and take longer in Southern California due to high contractor costs and permit requirements
  • You're paying carrying costs (insurance, property taxes, utilities) every month during probate
  • The longer you wait, the more stressed you become

Dealing with Tenants in Inherited Properties

If your inherited California property has tenants, you face additional complications. California's tenant-friendly laws, including strict eviction protections and rent control in many cities, make this particularly challenging.

You cannot simply remove tenants because you inherited the property. They retain their lease rights, and California's eviction process can take 6-12 months even under normal circumstances.

The California Probate Real Estate Sale Process

Once you have court authority to sell, the actual sales process has specific requirements under California law.

Working with Real Estate Agents

While you can sell the property yourself, most executors work with experienced probate real estate agents who understand the unique requirements. The agent must include specific language in the listing indicating it's a probate sale subject to court confirmation (if required).

In Orange County's competitive market, probate properties often attract investors and cash buyers who understand the process and won't be scared off by the additional requirements.

Disclosure Requirements

California's strict disclosure laws still apply to probate sales, though with some modifications. You must complete a Probate Real Property Disclosure statement, and if you have knowledge of property defects, you're obligated to disclose them.

However, if you never lived in the property and have limited knowledge of its condition, you can state that in your disclosures.

The Escrow Process

California probate sales use the same escrow process as traditional sales, but with additional steps. The escrow company must understand probate requirements, including:

  • Extended timelines for court approval
  • Potential for overbids at confirmation hearings
  • Specific disbursement requirements for estate debts
  • Coordination with the probate attorney

Experienced Orange County escrow companies handle these requirements regularly, especially in areas like Newport Beach, Laguna Beach, and Irvine where property values and probate sales are common.
