Understanding California Probate and Inherited Property

When you inherit property in California, the transfer process typically involves probate court unless the estate was held in a trust or the property was jointly owned. Orange County probate can take anywhere from 9 months to over a year, depending on the complexity of the estate and whether any disputes arise among heirs.

During probate, the court validates the will, appoints an executor or administrator, and oversees the distribution of assets. If you're ready to sell the inherited property, you'll generally need court approval before proceeding with the sale, unless the will specifically granted the executor independent administration powers.

Proposition 19 and Property Tax Implications

California's Proposition 19, which took effect in February 2021, significantly changed how inherited properties are taxed. Unlike the previous parent-child exclusion that allowed property tax bases to transfer without reassessment, Prop 19 now requires the inheriting child to use the home as their primary residence and limits the exemption to properties valued up to $1 million over the assessed value.

For most inherited properties in Orange County, where median home values in cities like Newport Beach, Laguna Beach, and Irvine often exceed $1-2 million, this means facing a substantial property tax reassessment. Many heirs discover their annual property taxes could increase by $10,000 to $30,000 or more, making it financially impractical to keep the property.

The True Costs of Keeping an Inherited Orange County Property

Beyond the emotional attachment, keeping an inherited property comes with significant ongoing expenses that many heirs don't initially consider:

  • Property taxes: Orange County property taxes average 1.1% of assessed value, which can mean $8,000-$20,000+ annually after reassessment
  • Insurance: California homeowners insurance has increased dramatically, with coastal properties facing even higher premiums
  • Maintenance and repairs: Older homes may need immediate attention, from roof repairs to foundation work
  • HOA fees: Many Orange County properties have monthly HOA fees ranging from $200-$800 or more
  • Utilities: Even vacant properties require basic utility services
  • Mortgage payments: If there's an existing mortgage, payments continue during probate

For properties in desirable but older neighborhoods like Garden Grove, Fullerton, or parts of Anaheim, deferred maintenance from an elderly parent's final years can mean $20,000-$50,000 in necessary repairs before the home is market-ready.

Options for Selling Your Inherited Orange County Property

Once you've decided to sell, you have several options, each with different timelines, costs, and levels of involvement.

Working with a real estate agent is the conventional route. In the hot Orange County market, well-prepared homes in areas like Mission Viejo, Lake Forest, or Huntington Beach can sell quickly and for top dollar. However, this option requires:

  • Preparing the home for sale (cleaning, repairs, staging)
  • Paying 5-6% in real estate commissions
  • Managing showings and open houses
  • Waiting 30-45 days for buyer financing and escrow
  • Risk of deals falling through due to inspection issues or financing problems

California-Specific Considerations When Selling Inherited Property

Dealing with Existing Tenants

If you've inherited a rental property in Orange County with existing tenants, California's strong tenant protection laws add another layer of complexity. You cannot simply force tenants out to prepare the property for sale. You must honor existing leases or provide proper notice according to California law, which can be 30-60 days or more depending on how long they've occupied the property.

Some cash buyers, including Golden Coast Cash Offer, will purchase properties with tenants in place, eliminating the need for you to navigate the eviction or notice process.

Capital Gains Tax and Step-Up in Basis

One financial advantage of inherited property is the step-up in basis. The property's tax basis is adjusted to its fair market value on the date of the original owner's death, not what they originally paid for it. This means if your parents bought their Costa Mesa home in 1980 for $150,000 and it's worth $1.2 million when you inherit it, your basis is $1.2 million.

If you sell relatively soon after inheriting, you'll have minimal or no capital gains tax. However, if you hold the property and it appreciates significantly before selling, you'll owe capital gains tax on the increase from the stepped-up basis.

Transfer and Recording Fees

Orange County charges specific fees for recording property transfers and deeds. While these are relatively modest compared to the property values involved, factor them into your planning along with title insurance and escrow fees, which are standard in all California real estate transactions.

When to Sell vs. Keep an Inherited Property

Despite the challenges, there are situations where keeping an inherited Orange County property makes sense:

  • You plan to move into it as your primary residence and can afford the new property tax basis
  • It generates strong rental income that exceeds all expenses, including the new tax assessment
  • You have strong sentimental attachment and clear financial ability to maintain it
  • The property is in a trust and wasn't subject to Prop 19 reassessment

However, for most heirs, particularly those who already own their own homes, live out of state, or inherit properties needing substantial work, selling makes the most financial and practical sense.
