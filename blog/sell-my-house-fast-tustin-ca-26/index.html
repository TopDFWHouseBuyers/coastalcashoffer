Understanding California Probate Law and Inherited Properties

When you inherit property in California, the first step is usually navigating the probate process. Probate is the legal procedure that transfers ownership from the deceased to the heirs, and in California, it can be particularly complex and time-consuming.

How Probate Works in Tustin

In Orange County, probate cases are handled through the Superior Court system. The process typically takes 9-18 months, though complicated estates can take even longer. During this time, you may be responsible for maintaining the property, paying property taxes, and covering utilities—all while you can't legally sell without court approval.

Some properties can avoid formal probate if they qualify for simplified procedures, such as when the estate value falls below California's threshold or when the property was held in a living trust. An experienced probate attorney can help determine which path applies to your situation.

California Property Tax Implications

California's Proposition 19, which took effect in February 2021, significantly changed how inherited properties are taxed. Previously, children could inherit their parents' low Proposition 13 property tax base. Now, that transfer only applies if the heir uses the home as their primary residence and the value doesn't exceed the original assessed value plus $1 million.

For most inherited properties in Tustin—where median home values exceed $900,000—this means the property will be reassessed at current market value upon transfer, potentially increasing annual property taxes by thousands of dollars. This reality makes selling quickly an attractive option for many heirs.

Steps to Selling Your Inherited Tustin Property

Step 1: Obtain Legal Authority to Sell

Before you can sell, you need legal authority. This might come through probate court approval, being named executor in a will, or being designated as successor trustee if the property was in a trust. Keep all documentation organized, as buyers and title companies will need proof of your authority to sell.

Step 2: Assess the Property's Condition

Many inherited homes in Tustin haven't been updated in years. Whether it's an older home in Columbus Tustin or a property near Tustin Legacy, you'll need to decide whether to sell as-is or invest in repairs. Consider that Tustin buyers often expect move-in ready homes, especially in competitive neighborhoods near excellent Tustin Unified schools.

Getting a professional inspection helps you understand what you're working with, though you're not obligated to make repairs if you choose to sell to a cash buyer.

Step 3: Determine Your Selling Strategy

You have several options for selling an inherited property in Tustin:

  • Traditional listing: Hiring a real estate agent to list on the MLS, which typically requires preparing the home, staging, and waiting for qualified buyers
  • Selling to a cash buyer: Working with a company that purchases homes as-is, with no repairs, inspections, or lengthy closings
  • Selling to an iBuyer: Using an instant-offer platform, though these often come with high fees and inspection contingencies

Each approach has advantages depending on your timeline, the property's condition, and whether you're managing the sale from out of state.

Step 4: Handle Existing Tenants (If Applicable)

If the inherited property has tenants, California's strict tenant protection laws apply. You cannot simply evict tenants to prepare for a sale. California's Tenant Protection Act of 2019 requires just cause for eviction, and Orange County has additional regulations protecting renters.

Many heirs find themselves in complicated situations with inherited rental properties in areas like Old Tustin, where long-term tenants may have been paying below-market rent. Cash buyers can purchase tenant-occupied properties, allowing you to sell without navigating eviction proceedings.

Tax Implications of Selling Inherited Property in California

Understanding the tax consequences helps you make informed decisions about timing and pricing.

Capital Gains Tax and Step-Up in Basis

Fortunately, inherited properties receive a "step-up in basis," meaning the property's value is reset to its fair market value on the date of the previous owner's death. If you sell relatively soon after inheriting, you'll likely owe little to no capital gains tax, even with Tustin's high property values.

However, if you hold the property for years and it appreciates significantly, you could face capital gains when you eventually sell. This is another reason many heirs choose to sell quickly.

Property Tax Reassessment

As mentioned earlier, Proposition 19 means most inherited properties will be reassessed at current market value. In Tustin, where property values have increased substantially over the past decades, this reassessment can result in property tax bills jumping from $3,000-$4,000 annually to $10,000-$12,000 or more.

If you don't plan to live in the property, these ongoing costs make selling quickly financially sensible.
