Current San Diego Market Conditions in 2026

San Diego continues to be one of California's most desirable real estate markets, characterized by strong demand and limited housing supply. The median home price in San Diego County remains significantly elevated compared to national averages, reflecting the premium buyers place on coastal proximity, year-round weather, and the region's thriving economy.

Several key factors are shaping the market:

  • Inventory levels remain relatively tight, particularly for well-maintained single-family homes
  • Interest rates have stabilized compared to recent years, bringing more qualified buyers to the market
  • Out-of-state buyers continue to view San Diego as an attractive destination, adding competitive pressure
  • Tech industry growth in nearby areas continues to drive demand from high-income professionals

Price Trends and Appreciation

San Diego home values have shown resilience through various economic cycles. While the dramatic appreciation rates of previous years have moderated, property values remain strong. Coastal neighborhoods like La Jolla, Del Mar, and Coronado continue commanding premium prices, while inland communities such as Santee, El Cajon, and Poway offer more accessible entry points while still benefiting from the San Diego market.

California-Specific Considerations for Sellers

Selling a home in California comes with unique regulatory and financial considerations that differ significantly from other states. Understanding these factors helps you budget appropriately and avoid surprises.

Proposition 19 and Property Tax Implications

California's Proposition 19, which took effect in 2021, continues to impact sellers in 2026. This law allows homeowners who are 55 or older, severely disabled, or victims of natural disasters to transfer their property tax base to a replacement home anywhere in California up to three times. If you qualify, this can result in significant tax savings when purchasing your next property.

Transfer Taxes and Closing Costs

San Diego County imposes transfer taxes on real estate sales, typically split between buyer and seller. The county transfer tax rate is $0.55 per $500 of property value. Additionally, some cities within San Diego County may impose their own transfer taxes. For a $900,000 home, expect county transfer taxes around $990, though your specific location may increase this amount.

California sellers should also budget for:

  • Escrow fees (typically 1-2% of sale price, split with buyer)
  • Title insurance for the buyer
  • Natural Hazard Disclosure reports (approximately $75-$200)
  • Any agreed-upon repairs or credits from inspections
  • Potential capital gains taxes if the property isn't your primary residence

Disclosure Requirements

California has some of the nation's most comprehensive disclosure requirements. Sellers must provide a Transfer Disclosure Statement (TDS) detailing the property's condition, along with Natural Hazard Disclosure statements regarding earthquake zones, flood zones, fire hazard areas, and other environmental concerns particularly relevant in Southern California.

The Traditional Selling Timeline in San Diego

Understanding the typical selling timeline helps you plan your move effectively. In the San Diego market, the traditional sale process usually unfolds like this:

Pre-Listing Preparation (2-4 weeks)

Most real estate agents recommend preparing your home before listing. This includes decluttering, making minor repairs, fresh paint, and possibly staging. In San Diego's competitive market, presentation matters significantly, especially in higher price ranges.

Active Marketing Period (30-60 days)

Well-priced homes in desirable San Diego neighborhoods typically receive offers within the first few weeks of listing. However, properties requiring extensive updates or priced above market may sit longer. The spring and summer months traditionally see the most buyer activity in Southern California.

Escrow and Closing (30-45 days)

California's escrow process is thorough. Once you accept an offer, expect 30-45 days to close, during which the buyer conducts inspections, secures financing, and reviews all disclosures. Be prepared for potential renegotiations if inspection issues arise.

Total timeline: 3-4 months from decision to sold

Challenges Facing San Diego Sellers in 2026

While San Diego remains a seller-friendly market in many ways, homeowners face several challenges when listing traditionally:

Buyer Inspection Contingencies

California buyers typically include extensive inspection periods in their offers. Even in competitive markets, most offers include 17-day inspection contingencies, during which buyers can request repairs or credits. Older San Diego homes may reveal foundation issues, outdated electrical systems, or deferred maintenance that leads to renegotiation.

Appraisal Gaps

With home prices near historic highs, appraisal gaps have become common. When a property doesn't appraise for the contracted price, buyers may need to bring additional cash or the deal may fall through entirely, sending you back to square one.

Selling Inherited or Distressed Properties

Many San Diego sellers face unique challenges: inherited properties requiring extensive updates, homes in probate, properties with tenant situations, or houses needing significant repairs. These situations often make traditional sales complicated and time-consuming.

Maximizing Your Net Proceeds

Smart San Diego sellers focus on net proceeds rather than just listing price. Here's how to calculate what you'll actually walk away with:

Sale Price
Minus: Agent commissions (typically 5-6% total)
Minus: Transfer taxes and recording fees
Minus: Escrow and title fees
Minus: Repairs and credits from inspection
Minus: Outstanding mortgage balance
Minus: Any property tax prorations or HOA fees
Equals: Your net proceeds

For a $900,000 San Diego home with a $400,000 mortgage remaining, traditional selling costs might include $54,000 in commissions, $2,000 in transfer taxes, $3,000 in escrow/title fees, and potentially $10,000-$20,000 in repairs and credits—reducing your equity by $69,000-$79,000 before you receive proceeds.

Tax Considerations

California doesn't impose a state-level capital gains tax separate from income tax, but capital gains are taxed as regular income at California's progressive rates. However, if you've lived in your home as your primary residence for at least two of the past five years, you can exclude up to $250,000 in gains ($500,000 for married couples) from federal taxation. Consult with a tax professional to understand your specific situation.

Neighborhood-Specific Insights

San Diego County encompasses diverse markets, each with unique characteristics:

Coastal Communities

Areas like La Jolla, Del Mar, Encinitas, and Coronado command the highest prices and attract affluent buyers seeking beach access and ocean views. Competition remains fierce, and well-presented properties sell quickly.

Urban Core

Downtown San Diego, North Park, and Hillcrest attract younger professionals and investors. Condos and townhomes dominate these areas, with walkability and nightlife as key selling points.

North County

Carlsbad, Oceanside, Vista, and Escondido offer more affordable options while maintaining proximity to beaches and employment centers. Family-friendly amenities and good schools drive demand.

East County

El Cajon, Santee, and La Mesa provide the most accessible price points in the county, attracting first-time buyers and families seeking value and space.
