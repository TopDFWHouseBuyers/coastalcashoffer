Selling a House With Code Violations in California

Published July 11, 2026 · Southern California
Back to All Articles

Discovering code violations on your Southern California property can feel overwhelming, especially when you're trying to sell. Whether it's unpermitted additions, electrical issues, or zoning problems, these violations can complicate a traditional sale—but they don't have to stop you from moving forward.

In California's competitive real estate market, code violations are more common than you might think. Many Orange County homeowners find themselves dealing with unpermitted work from previous owners or improvements that were completed without proper permits. Understanding your options can help you make the best decision for your situation.

Understanding California Code Violations

Code violations in California occur when property improvements don't meet local building codes or lack proper permits. Common violations in Southern California include:

  • Unpermitted room additions or converted garages
  • Electrical or plumbing work done without permits
  • Zoning violations or illegal ADUs
  • Safety hazards like faulty wiring or structural issues
  • Setback violations or encroachments

California's strict building codes exist for good reason—they protect homeowners and ensure safety. However, when you inherit these problems or discover them before selling, they can create significant obstacles in the traditional real estate market.

The Cost of Fixing Code Violations in California

Bringing your property up to code in Southern California isn't cheap. Depending on the violation, you could face:

  • Permit application fees ranging from $500 to $5,000+
  • Contractor costs to complete or modify work ($10,000-$50,000 or more)
  • Inspection fees and potential fines
  • Architectural or engineering reports ($2,000-$10,000)
  • Time delays of 3-6 months or longer

In high-value markets like Irvine, Newport Beach, and Mission Viejo, these costs can quickly escalate. Orange County building departments have particularly stringent requirements, and the permitting process can be lengthy and complex.

Can You Sell a House With Code Violations in California?

Yes, you can legally sell a house with code violations in California, but you must disclose them. California Civil Code Section 1102 requires sellers to complete a Transfer Disclosure Statement (TDS) that includes known defects and violations.

Challenges With Traditional Sales

Most traditional buyers in the California market will struggle to purchase a home with code violations because:

  • Lenders typically won't finance properties with significant violations
  • Home inspections will flag the issues, often killing deals
  • Buyers may demand extensive repairs or credits
  • Title companies may refuse to insure the property
  • The appraisal may come in low due to violations

These complications can leave your property sitting on the market for months while you continue paying property taxes, insurance, and maintenance costs.

Other Options for Selling With Violations

Fix the Violations Before Selling

If you have the financial resources and time, resolving violations before listing can maximize your sale price in California's strong real estate market. This makes sense if the violations are minor and the cost to fix is substantially less than the potential price increase.

Reduce Your Asking Price

Some sellers price their homes lower to account for violations, hoping to attract investors or contractors. However, you'll still face disclosure requirements and potential buyer financing issues.

Sell to an Investor or Flipper

Real estate investors often purchase properties with violations, though they'll factor repair costs into their offers. This can still be faster than the traditional market but may involve more negotiation.
