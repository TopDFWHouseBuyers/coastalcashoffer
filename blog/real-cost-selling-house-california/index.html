The Real Cost of Selling a House in California

Published October 05, 2026 · Southern California
Back to All Articles

Selling a home in Southern California? You're looking at one of the hottest real estate markets in the country, but those high home values come with equally high selling costs. Most California homeowners are surprised to learn that selling expenses typically range from 8-10% of the sale price—and sometimes even more depending on your situation.

If you're planning to sell your Orange County or Southern California home, understanding the full financial picture is crucial before you list. Let's break down exactly what you'll pay when selling a house in California, so you can make informed decisions about your next move.

California Real Estate Agent Commissions

The single largest expense most California home sellers face is the real estate commission. In Southern California, the traditional commission structure typically runs between 5-6% of the final sale price, split between the listing agent and buyer's agent.

Here's what that means in real numbers for Orange County homeowners:

  • Home selling for $800,000: $40,000-$48,000 in commissions
  • Home selling for $1,200,000: $60,000-$72,000 in commissions
  • Home selling for $1,500,000: $75,000-$90,000 in commissions

With Orange County's median home price hovering around $1 million, that commission alone can easily exceed $50,000. While commissions are technically negotiable, most traditional agents in competitive markets like Irvine, Newport Beach, and Mission Viejo stick close to these standard rates.

California Transfer Taxes and Documentary Fees

California charges specific taxes when property ownership transfers from seller to buyer. These vary significantly by location within Southern California.

County Transfer Taxes

Most California counties charge $1.10 per $1,000 of the sale price. On an $800,000 home, that's $880. However, some cities add their own transfer taxes on top of the county rate:

  • Los Angeles City: Additional $4.50 per $1,000
  • Santa Monica: Additional $3.00 per $1,000 for properties over $5 million
  • Most Orange County cities: Stick to the base county rate

While Orange County generally has lower transfer taxes than Los Angeles, you'll want to verify the exact rate for your specific city. In San Clemente, Dana Point, or Laguna Beach, you're typically looking at just the base county rate, which is relatively reasonable compared to other parts of California.

Title Insurance and Escrow Fees

California operates on an escrow system, which differs from many other states. In Southern California, it's customary for the seller to pay for the owner's title insurance policy for the buyer.

Title insurance costs in California are regulated and based on the sale price:

  • $500,000 home: Approximately $1,500-$2,000
  • $800,000 home: Approximately $2,200-$2,800
  • $1,200,000 home: Approximately $3,000-$3,800

Escrow fees typically run $2-$3 per $1,000 of the sale price, plus a base fee of around $250-$500. These fees are often split 50/50 between buyer and seller, though this is negotiable.

Property Repairs and Pre-Sale Improvements

California's disclosure laws are among the strictest in the nation. Sellers must complete a Transfer Disclosure Statement (TDS) detailing known issues with the property. Once disclosed, buyers will likely request repairs or credits.

Common repair costs for California home sellers include:

  • Roof repairs or replacement: $5,000-$25,000+
  • Foundation issues (common in older Southern California homes): $3,000-$15,000+
  • Plumbing updates: $1,500-$8,000
  • HVAC repairs: $500-$5,000
  • Termite treatment and repairs (required for many California loans): $1,000-$3,500

Additionally, in competitive markets like Orange County, many sellers invest in pre-sale staging ($2,000-$5,000) and cosmetic updates to maximize their sale price.

Home Inspection and Reports

While not always required, savvy California sellers often obtain pre-listing inspections and reports to avoid surprises during the buyer's inspection period:

  • General home inspection: $400-$600
  • Pest inspection: $75-$150
  • Roof inspection: $200-$400
  • Sewer line inspection: $250-$500
  • Natural Hazard Disclosure Report (required in California): $75-$200

In high-value areas like Newport Beach, Laguna Hills, and Irvine, investing in these reports upfront can prevent deals from falling apart and give buyers confidence.

Mortgage Payoff and Prepayment Penalties

If you still have a mortgage on your California home, you'll need to pay it off at closing. While most mortgages today don't include prepayment penalties, some loans—particularly those originated before 2014—may charge a fee for early payoff.

You'll also need to account for:

  • Remaining principal balance
  • Prorated interest up to the closing date
  • Any home equity loans or HELOCs on the property
  • Potential IRS liens (if applicable)

California is a non-judicial foreclosure state, which means if you're behind on payments and selling to avoid foreclosure, the process moves relatively quickly. Working with a cash buyer can expedite the sale and help you avoid foreclosure damage to your credit.

Prorated Property Taxes

California's property tax system operates on a fiscal year from July 1 to June 30. Thanks to Proposition 13, property taxes are capped at 1% of the assessed value plus any local bonds and assessments (typically bringing the total to 1.1-1.25%).

At closing, you'll pay prorated property taxes for the portion of the tax year you owned the home. On a $1 million Orange County home, annual property taxes run approximately $11,000-$12,500. If you close in March, you'll owe roughly 9 months of taxes, or about $8,250-$9,400.

HOA Fees and Transfer Fees

Many Southern California homes—especially in master-planned communities throughout Irvine, Aliso Viejo, and Lake Forest—are part of homeowners associations. When selling, you're responsible for:

  • Prorated HOA dues through closing
  • HOA transfer fees: $200-$800
  • HOA document preparation fees: $100-$400
  • Outstanding HOA violations or special assessments

Some Orange County communities have multiple HOA layers (master association plus sub-association), which can double these fees.

Capital Gains Taxes (Sometimes)

Most California homeowners who've lived in their primary residence for at least two of the past five years can exclude up to $250,000 (single) or $500,000 (married) in capital gains from federal taxes. However, if your profits exceed these thresholds—entirely possible in Southern California's appreciating market—you'll owe capital gains tax on the excess.

California also imposes state capital gains tax, treating profits as ordinary income. With California's top income tax rate at 13.3%, this can significantly impact high-appreciation sales.

Miscellaneous Selling Costs

Don't forget these additional expenses California home sellers commonly face:

  • Moving and storage costs: $1,500-$5,000+
  • Home warranty for buyer (often negotiated): $400-$600
  • Final utility bills and deposits
  • Cleaning services: $200-$500
  • Landscaping touch-ups: $300-$1,000
  • Attorney fees (if complications arise): $1,500-$5,000+

The Total Cost: A Real Example

Let's look at what a typical Orange County home seller might pay when selling a $1,000,000 home through traditional channels:

  • Real estate commissions (5.5%): $55,000
  • Title insurance: $3,200
  • Escrow fees: $1,500
  • Transfer taxes: $1,100
  • Repairs and improvements: $8,000
  • Pre-listing inspections: $1,000
  • Prorated property taxes: $5,600
  • HOA fees and transfers: $800
  • Miscellaneous costs: $2,500

Total: $78,700 (7.87% of sale price)

This doesn't include any mortgage payoff or capital gains taxes. When you factor in everything, selling a home in California through traditional methods typically costs 8-10% of the sale price—sometimes even more.
