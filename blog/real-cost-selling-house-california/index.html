The Real Cost of Selling a House in California

Published May 30, 2026 · Southern California
Back to All Articles

Thinking about selling your California home? While our state's strong real estate market means solid equity for most homeowners, the costs of selling can quickly add up to much more than you'd expect. Understanding these expenses upfront helps you plan better and avoid surprises at closing.

In California, the total cost of selling a house typically ranges from 8% to 10% of your home's sale price. For a median Orange County home valued at $1,000,000, that means you could be looking at $80,000 to $100,000 in selling costs. Let's break down exactly where that money goes.

Real Estate Agent Commissions: The Biggest Expense

Real estate agent commissions represent the largest cost when selling your California home, typically totaling 5% to 6% of the sale price. This commission is usually split between your listing agent and the buyer's agent.

On a $1,000,000 home in Irvine or Newport Beach, a 6% commission equals $60,000. While this fee is technically negotiable, it's become standard practice throughout Southern California and helps ensure your home gets maximum market exposure.

California Transfer Taxes and Recording Fees

California has specific transfer tax requirements that vary by county and city. In Orange County, you'll pay a county transfer tax of $1.10 per $1,000 of the sale price. Some cities add their own transfer taxes on top of the county rate.

  • Orange County transfer tax: $1.10 per $1,000
  • Recording fees: typically $50-$250
  • City transfer taxes vary (some areas have none, others can be substantial)

For example, selling a $750,000 home in Mission Viejo means paying approximately $825 in county transfer taxes alone.

Title Insurance and Escrow Fees

California traditionally follows a practice where sellers pay for the buyer's title insurance policy, though this is negotiable. Title insurance costs roughly $1,000 to $3,000 depending on your home's value.

Escrow fees in Southern California typically run $2 to $3 per $1,000 of the sales price, plus a base fee. These costs are often split between buyer and seller, but California allows this to be negotiated in the purchase agreement.

What Escrow Covers

Your escrow fees handle the neutral third-party management of funds, document preparation, and coordination between all parties. In California's complex real estate market, this service is essential for protecting both parties.

Home Preparation and Repair Costs

California buyers expect move-in ready homes, especially in competitive markets like Orange County and San Diego. Typical preparation costs include:

  • Pre-listing home inspection: $400-$600
  • Repairs identified in inspections: $1,000-$10,000+
  • Professional staging: $2,000-$5,000 for vacant homes
  • Deep cleaning: $300-$600
  • Landscaping improvements: $500-$3,000

Many sellers in Laguna Hills, Lake Forest, and surrounding areas spend $5,000 to $15,000 getting their homes market-ready.

California Property Tax Proration

California's property taxes are paid in arrears, meaning you're always paying for a period you've already lived in the home. At closing, you'll need to pay your share of property taxes through your final day of ownership. This is calculated and prorated through escrow.

Thanks to Proposition 13, California property taxes are capped at 1% of assessed value plus local assessments, but the proration at closing can still represent a significant amount depending on your closing date.

Additional Potential Costs

Depending on your situation, you may encounter these additional expenses:

  • HOA transfer fees: $200-$500 in communities throughout Orange County
  • Natural Hazard Disclosure reports: $100-$300 (required in California)
  • Mortgage payoff penalties: varies by lender and loan terms
  • Attorney fees: optional in California but recommended in complex situations
  • Capital gains taxes: if not your primary residence or you've owned it less than 2 years

The Hidden Cost: Time and Stress

Beyond the financial costs, traditional home sales in California take an average of 30-45 days to close, and that's after you've found a buyer. Factor in preparation time, showings, negotiations, and potential deal fall-throughs, and you're looking at months of your life dedicated to the selling process.

California's strict disclosure laws also mean you're legally obligated to reveal any known defects, which can complicate negotiations and extend your timeline even further.

How to Reduce Your Selling Costs

While some costs are unavoidable in traditional sales, you have options:

  1. Negotiate commission rates with agents (though this may impact service quality)
  2. Handle minor repairs yourself if you have the skills
  3. Time your sale to minimize property tax proration
