Falling behind on property taxes in California can feel overwhelming, especially with Orange County's high property values. The good news? You have options, and selling your house might be the fastest way to resolve back taxes while protecting your remaining equity.

Property tax liens in California are serious business. The state gives delinquent taxes priority over almost all other debts, meaning they get paid first when you sell. But understanding the process and your timeline can help you make the best decision for your situation.

Understanding California Property Tax Liens

In California, property taxes become delinquent on June 30th if the second installment remains unpaid. After five years of delinquency, the county can initiate tax sale proceedings. Orange County, Los Angeles County, and San Diego County are particularly vigilant about collecting overdue taxes due to their high property values.

Here's what happens with unpaid property taxes in California:

  • A 10% penalty is added immediately when taxes become delinquent
  • An additional 1.5% penalty accrues monthly on the unpaid balance
  • After five years, the property becomes subject to the county tax collector's power to sell
  • The tax lien takes priority over mortgages, home equity loans, and most other liens

Can You Sell a House With Back Taxes?

Absolutely. In fact, selling is often the smartest move to avoid losing your property at a tax sale auction. When you sell a house in California, back taxes are paid from the proceeds at closing, just like your mortgage balance.

The California escrow process makes this straightforward. Your escrow company will request a property tax statement from the county, calculate the exact amount owed including penalties and interest, and pay it directly from your sale proceeds before you receive any money.

The key is acting before the county initiates tax sale proceedings, which can reduce your control over the process and timing.
