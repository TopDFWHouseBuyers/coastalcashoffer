Understanding California's Probate Process

When you inherit property in San Juan Capistrano, the first hurdle is often California's probate process. Probate is the legal procedure that transfers ownership from the deceased to their heirs. In California, this process can take anywhere from 9 months to 2 years, depending on the complexity of the estate.

During probate, the executor must inventory assets, pay outstanding debts, and eventually distribute property to beneficiaries. If you're looking to sell the inherited home, you'll typically need court approval unless the property was held in a trust or transferred through other means that bypass probate.

Ways to Expedite the Process

  • Check if the property was held in a living trust
  • Consider using California's simplified probate procedures for smaller estates
  • Work with a probate attorney familiar with Orange County courts
  • Gather all necessary documentation early, including the death certificate and will

California Property Tax Implications for Inherited Homes

California's property tax landscape changed significantly with Proposition 19, which took effect in February 2021. Understanding these changes is critical when inheriting property in San Juan Capistrano, where median home values exceed $1 million in many neighborhoods.

Under the new rules, inherited properties are reassessed at current market value unless the heir plans to use it as their primary residence and the added value doesn't exceed $1 million over the original assessed value. For many San Juan Capistrano properties, this means a substantial increase in property taxes if you inherit a home your parents purchased decades ago.

This tax reassessment often motivates heirs to sell rather than keep inherited properties, especially if they already own a primary residence elsewhere in Southern California.

Dealing with Tenants in Inherited San Juan Capistrano Properties

If your inherited property has tenants, you'll need to navigate California's tenant-friendly laws, which are among the strictest in the nation. San Juan Capistrano falls under California's statewide rent control and just-cause eviction protections for properties over 15 years old.

You cannot simply ask tenants to leave because you've inherited the property. You must either honor existing leases or provide proper notice under California law. For month-to-month tenancies, you typically need to provide 60 days' notice if the tenant has lived there over a year, and you may owe relocation assistance depending on local ordinances.

Many traditional buyers shy away from tenant-occupied properties, which can limit your selling options and extend your timeline considerably.

Capital Gains Tax Considerations

When you sell an inherited property in California, you'll potentially owe capital gains tax on the appreciation from the date you inherited it. Fortunately, inherited properties receive a "step-up in basis," meaning the property's tax basis is reset to its fair market value at the time of the original owner's death.

For example, if your parents bought their San Juan Capistrano home for $200,000 in 1985, and it was worth $1.2 million when you inherited it in 2024, your basis is $1.2 million. If you sell for $1.25 million, you'd only owe capital gains on $50,000, not the full appreciation.

This step-up in basis is one reason many financial advisors recommend selling inherited properties relatively quickly, before additional appreciation occurs. Consulting with a California tax professional familiar with Orange County real estate can help you understand your specific situation.
