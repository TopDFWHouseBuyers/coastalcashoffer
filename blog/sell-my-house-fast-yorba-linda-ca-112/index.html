California-Specific Considerations When Selling

Selling property in California comes with unique legal and financial considerations that differ from other states.

California Escrow Requirements

California requires neutral third-party escrow for real estate transactions, which protects both buyers and sellers. The escrow process typically takes 30-45 days for traditional financed sales, but cash transactions can close much faster since there's no lender approval required.

Property Tax Implications

Under California law, property taxes are reassessed when ownership changes. As a seller, your taxes are prorated to the closing date. Any supplemental tax bills that arrive after closing are the buyer's responsibility.

Disclosure Requirements

California has strict seller disclosure requirements, including the Transfer Disclosure Statement (TDS) and Natural Hazard Disclosure. When working with Golden Coast Cash Offer, we're experienced with California disclosure laws and make the process straightforward, even if your property has issues that would concern traditional buyers.

Title and Liens

Before closing, any liens on the property must be addressed. This includes mortgages, property tax liens, HOA liens, and mechanic's liens. The escrow company will ensure all liens are paid from the sale proceeds, and you'll receive the net amount after these obligations are satisfied.
